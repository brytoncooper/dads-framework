# Dad

## Daddy issues

If your old codebase were a girl, her name would be Tess. She can run payroll
in three countries. She also keeps a feature flag called `temporary` because
the guy who added it said he’d be right back.

That was six years ago. Sometimes she bumps his ticket.

Tess has daddy issues. You can tell how she was raised by what happens when
you try to change her email provider.

## Anxious attachment

“Are you leaving me?” she asks the vendor. He’s renaming one field. She has
his response type in eleven packages and his last name on her checkout page.

## Avoidant attachment

Ask Tess what her own endpoint returns and suddenly she doesn’t like labels.
“It’s an object.” Sweetheart, the mobile app has been waiting four years.

## Disorganized attachment

At the front door she checks your ID. Round the back she’ll believe anything
in a JSON body. Both doors lead to the same refund.

## Enmeshment

Billing goes through Shipping’s bedroom to borrow a private helper. Shipping
moves the furniture. Payroll goes down in Belgium.

## You are looking for a father figure

She brings home a new ORM. “He has _very_ strong opinions.” By Sunday he’s
named all the children.

## You repeat the cycle

Tess checks membership before showing you a task. The activity feed has its
own little version of membership. Kick somebody out and they can still read
the meeting notes. Guess which endpoint nobody tested.

## You let the babysitter raise them

The coding agent found six near-identical permission helpers. It wanted to
respect local conventions. There are seven now.

## People-pleasing

She hates saying no. “You can’t open Secret Acquisition Plan,” says the API
to a stranger who hadn’t known there was a Secret Acquisition Plan.

## You stay with people who cannot meet you

The vendor has stopped returning her calls. He still gets a vote on her Node
version. She calls the arrangement a peer dependency.

## Commitment issues

Tess will save the note. Writing down who owns it feels like a big step.

## Gaslighting

“All the tests passed.” Tess, the database never started.

## The silent treatment

TypeScript tried to tell her. There’s a little comment over its mouth.

## Failure to launch

Her refund policy still lives in the HTTP handler. To ask it a question,
you have to open a port.

## “It’s not you. It’s my boundaries.”

Then somebody does one decent thing for Tess. They give Notes its own
contract. The customer API mounts the notes routes. The staff API chooses
its own. For once, being in the same repo doesn’t mean sharing a toothbrush.

## The paper wall

Before that, somebody had put the database row in a folder called
`adapters` and exported it unchanged. Tess had been paying rent on a curtain.

## Dad has issues too

Dad’s first instinct is to buy a label maker. Take it away from him.
Tess needs a door on her room. She does not need an interface for the knob.

In the [Workbench](apps/workbench/README.md), remove a member and check the
task, the artifacts, _and the feed_. That’s the part Dad can do for her.
Somebody finally came back and finished the ticket.
